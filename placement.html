Traffic × Intent

Not All Foot Traffic Is Worth the Same

Volume alone does not sell flowers. The zones that convert are the ones where high foot traffic overlaps with a reason to arrive carrying something. The upper-right quadrant is where a unit earns its medium and high tier numbers; the lower-right moves people past the glass without giving them an occasion.
Zone inside the prime placement window Zone outside it — traffic without a matching occasion Prime placement zone: high traffic × high intent
Emergency department waiting is the trap: it posts the highest raw counts in the building and the lowest reason to buy. People arrive there without an occasion.
Timing

When the Building Is Actually Buying

Placement sets the ceiling; timing sets how often that ceiling gets hit. Three patterns matter — the evening visitor window, the shift-change surges, and the hours the gift shop is not open to serve either of them.
5–8 pm
Peak visitor window — the densest arrival block of the day, and the one most likely to arrive empty-handed
7a · 3p · 5p · 7p · 11p
All-shift surge times — five distinct movement peaks as the building changes over
~115 hrs/wk
Hours the gift shop sits dark — demand that arrives outside staffed retail hours has nowhere to go

The surge stat covers the whole workforce

The five surge times are not day-shift only. Taken together they capture every shift change in the building — clinical and non-clinical, days, evenings and overnight. A unit placed on a main circulation path sees all five, which is what separates a machine that is available from a machine that is passed.

Where We Are Honest About the Model

Two Caveats Worth Stating Up Front

The grid above is a working read, not a measured result. Two points in it are assumptions we would rather name now than defend later.
The maternity rank is a pilot hypothesis, not a measured result. Placing maternity and the women’s center at the top of the intent axis reflects our reading of who walks that corridor and why. It has not been validated against unit-level sales data. The first placements should be structured to test it, and the ranking should move if the data says it belongs elsewhere.
Visiting hours close around 8 pm. The 5–8 pm visitor window ends where visiting hours end, so the tail of that block is truncated rather than open-ended. The later surge times (7 pm and 11 pm) are staff movement, not visitors, and should be modeled as a different buyer with a different basket.
Zone rankings are working estimates based on circulation patterns and stated assumptions, not measured sales data. Actual results will vary by building, layout, product mix, and operational execution. This document is not a guarantee of performance.
